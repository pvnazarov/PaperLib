## Abstract

A previously unannotated oncogenic splicing-derived isoform of EVI1 is identified, frequently present in inv(3)/t(3;3) AML and directly contributing to leukemic transformation. The isoform is generated by oncogenic mutations in the core splicing factor SF3B1, mutated in over 30% of inv(3)/t(3;3) myeloid neoplasm patients and the single most commonly co-occurring alteration in that group; SF3B1 mutations are statistically uniquely enriched in these patients. Combined expression of the SF3B1 mutation with the human inv(3) allele in mice enhanced myeloid lineage skewing, HSPC expansion and leukemia development, and the mis-splicing event was shared across human and murine SF3B1-mutant samples.

## Summary

Explains a striking co-mutation pattern by showing that the two lesions are not independent. inv(3)/t(3;3) drives EVI1 through enhancer rearrangement, and SF3B1 mutation - uniquely enriched in these patients - produces a specific mis-spliced EVI1 isoform that is itself oncogenic. So the second mutation is selected because it modifies the product of the first.

The cross-species control is the elegant part: mis-splicing of EVI1 was seen in mice only when the human EVI1 sequence was present in mutant SF3B1 cells, which both demonstrates sequence dependence and validates the humanised model. That mutant SF3B1 adds a distinct cancer program on top of inv(3), rather than simply accelerating it, is supported by the enhanced lineage skewing and expansion in the combined model.

## Key points

- A novel, unannotated EVI1 splice isoform is frequent in inv(3)/t(3;3) AML and contributes directly to transformation.
- It is generated by SF3B1 mutations, which are uniquely enriched in this subtype and the most common co-occurring alteration.
- Explains the co-mutation pattern: the second lesion is selected because it modifies the product of the first.
- Combined SF3B1 mutation and human inv(3) allele in mice enhanced lineage skewing, HSPC expansion and leukemia development.
- Mis-splicing occurred only when the human EVI1 sequence was present, confirming sequence dependence.

## Limitations

The authors set out what remains unresolved: the clinical impact of coexisting SF3B1 mutations and distinct EVI1 isoforms needs larger patient cohorts with paired bulk and single-cell genomic data; how different breakpoints produce varying levels of EVI1 upregulation is unstudied; and because SF3B1 mutations often arise in clonal hematopoiesis, the order of acquisition relative to the chromosome 3 rearrangement is unknown - which matters for whether this is cooperation or sequential selection. The mouse models require the human EVI1 sequence to be introduced, so they are engineered rather than natural. No therapeutic strategy follows directly, since neither the isoform nor mutant SF3B1 is currently druggable in this context.
