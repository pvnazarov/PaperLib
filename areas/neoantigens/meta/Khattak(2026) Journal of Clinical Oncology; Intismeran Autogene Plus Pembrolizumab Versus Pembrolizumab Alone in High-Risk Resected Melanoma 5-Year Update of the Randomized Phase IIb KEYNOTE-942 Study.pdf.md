## Abstract

Five-year outcomes from KEYNOTE-942, a randomised phase IIb trial of the individualised mRNA neoantigen therapy intismeran autogene plus pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab alone in resected stage IIIB-IV melanoma (n=157, randomised 2:1). At a median 60.3 months, recurrence-free survival HR 0.510 (95% CI 0.294-0.887) and distant metastasis-free survival HR 0.411 (0.200-0.843) favoured the combination.

## Summary

This is the strongest clinical evidence in the collection, and the only randomised one: the neoantigen vaccine papers elsewhere here are single-arm phase I studies. A randomised comparison against an active control that holds at five years is a different order of evidence.

The overall survival hazard ratio of 0.471 has a confidence interval crossing 1 (0.165-1.345), so it is described as a favourable trend rather than a result - the authors are explicit that OS data remain immature.

## Key points

- Randomised, with an active comparator (pembrolizumab alone), unlike every other vaccine study in this collection.
- RFS HR 0.510 and DMFS HR 0.411 both exclude 1 at five years' median follow-up.
- Overall survival HR 0.471 but CI 0.165-1.345 - a trend, not a finding, and stated as such.
- Safety remained manageable without the added toxicity of conventional combination immunotherapy.

## Limitations

The authors list them: a relatively small phase II sample, immature overall survival data, and exclusion of patients who had prior neoadjuvant or perioperative systemic therapy, which limits generalisability to how melanoma is now commonly treated. They also state that further work is needed to confirm the functional specificity of the T-cell responses and to define biomarkers of response - so the mechanism by which the benefit arises is not established here. Five-year analyses were descriptive, not formally powered.
