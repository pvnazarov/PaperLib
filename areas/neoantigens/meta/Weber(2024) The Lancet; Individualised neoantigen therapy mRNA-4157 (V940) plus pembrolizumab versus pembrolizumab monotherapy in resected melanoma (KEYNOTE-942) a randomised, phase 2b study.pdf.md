## Abstract

The primary report of KEYNOTE-942, a randomised phase 2b trial of individualised neoantigen therapy mRNA-4157 (V940) plus pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab alone in resected high-risk melanoma. Recurrence-free survival favoured the combination.

## Summary

This is the randomised evidence that the neoantigen vaccine field had lacked - every other vaccine study in this collection is single-arm - and the five-year update by Khattak, also here, extends it.

The rationale stated is that individualised therapy might overcome the limitations of earlier shared-antigen vaccines, by increasing endogenous neoantigen T-cell responses, inducing epitope spreading, and broadening recognition of neoantigen-encoded epitopes on the tumour surface.

## Key points

- Randomised against an active comparator, the first such trial for an individualised neoantigen vaccine.
- Recurrence-free survival benefit for the combination in resected high-risk melanoma.
- Mechanistic rationale is threefold: boost endogenous responses, induce epitope spreading, broaden surface epitope recognition.
- The basis for the five-year update reported by Khattak (2026) in this collection.

## Limitations

The authors report a consequential enrolment irregularity: because of COVID-19-related manufacturing limitations, some patients randomly assigned to combination therapy were manually reallocated to the monotherapy group, and once manufacturing resumed all remaining patients went to the combination arm. They state baseline characteristics were balanced overall and in the pre- and post-February-2021 subpopulations, but this is a departure from randomisation and should be weighed. Phase 2b, and overall survival was immature at this report.
