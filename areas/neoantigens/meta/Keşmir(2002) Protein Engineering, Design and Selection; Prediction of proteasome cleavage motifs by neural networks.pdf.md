## Abstract

A neural network predicting proteasomal cleavage - the degradation step that generates the fragments capable of binding MHC class I. Unlike earlier algorithms trained on in vitro digestion by constitutive proteasomes, this one accounts for the immunoproteasome, and the authors combine cleavage, TAP transport and MHC binding into a single quality-of-presentation score.

## Summary

The paper is the origin of a design still standard today: model the antigen processing pathway as a chain of probabilities rather than treating MHC binding as the whole problem. Their formulation defines the probability of generating an epitope and then divides through by TAP and MHC affinities to give a composite score.

The distinction between constitutive and immunoproteasome matters because the two cleave differently and the immunoproteasome is what operates in cells under interferon exposure - which is the state a tumour under immune attack is in.

## Key points

- Trained with immunoproteasome data rather than constitutive-proteasome digestion alone.
- Combines the three steps - degradation, transport, binding - into one quality-of-presentation score.
- Cleavage-promoting and cleavage-inhibiting motifs are extracted from the network weights, giving interpretable output.
- The upstream companion to the binding predictors that dominate this collection.

## Limitations

The authors state two constraints plainly: they analysed only immunoproteasome-generated peptides that bind MHC, not all peptides generated, so the immunoproteasome result must be interpreted with caution; and because the N-terminus is generated by different proteolytic processes, their model does not account for generating it correctly - only the C-terminus. Training data come from in vitro digestion of a limited set of substrates.
