## Abstract

The thymoproteasome subunit beta5T, expressed only in cortical thymic epithelial cells, generates a distinct set of peptide-MHC class I complexes specialised for positive selection of CD8 T cells. The peptides produced differ from those generated by the standard and immuno-proteasomes found elsewhere.

## Summary

Positive selection requires that double-positive thymocytes engage self-pMHC on cortical thymic epithelium with low affinity; too strong and they are deleted, too weak and they die by neglect. This paper identifies part of the machinery that makes cTEC ligands special: a proteasome subunit restricted to that cell type, producing a peptide repertoire found nowhere else.

That is the mechanistic underpinning of the Koncz argument elsewhere in this collection - if the peptides mediating positive selection are generated by distinct machinery, the resulting repertoire is shaped by that machinery's cleavage preferences.

## Key points

- beta5T is expressed only in cortical thymic epithelial cells and produces a peptide repertoire distinct from standard and immunoproteasomes.
- Those peptides are functionally specialised for positive selection, not merely different.
- Explains why the ligands shaping the naive repertoire are not a random sample of the self proteome.
- Provides the mechanism behind repertoire-level constraints on which foreign motifs can ever be recognised.

## Limitations

Mouse thymus: the human thymoproteasome exists but the peptide repertoires and their selection consequences are not established to be equivalent. The work identifies a specialised repertoire without fully characterising which peptides it contains, so it cannot yet be used to predict which TCR specificities will be positively selected. Its relevance to neoantigen prediction is mechanistic and indirect.
