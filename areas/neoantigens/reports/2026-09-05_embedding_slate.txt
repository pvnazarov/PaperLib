Embedding slate, 2026-09-05 -- every result, losers included.

WHY: the owner reported the map looked close to random, with the newest papers
pushed to the edge. Two causes, both confirmed by measurement:

  1. The TF-IDF vocabulary was fitted on the FIRST 60 papers and frozen -- 585
     terms. The subject matter of the 57 papers added later was ABSENT from it:
     proteasome, proteasomal, erap1, trimming, cleavage, splicing, transporter,
     indel, noncanonical. Those papers could only be positioned by the generic
     words they shared with batch 1 ("binding", "prediction", "cancer"), which is
     the failure mode embed.py's own comments name.
  2. They were placed by umap.transform() into a layout fitted on the old 60.
     Out-of-sample points land at the periphery. Near-featureless vectors plus
     outside projection is exactly "new papers on the edge".

Neither is a design flaw: the sha256 layout cache exists to stop the map
reshuffling, and it did its job. It assumes a few papers are added to a stable
collection. This one nearly doubled.

MEASURE: top-5 same-topic agreement in the FULL vector space -- for every paper,
its five nearest by cosine, ignoring the display threshold. The 18 topics were
drawn by READING and are independent of the vectors, so this is not the circular
cohesion measure eval/reclustering.json rules out. Same k, all 117 papers, every
candidate. Part-level (5 parts) is the coarser check.

  model / input                     topic(18)   part(5)   separation   coverage
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Tier 0 TF-IDF, frozen (was live)     n/a        n/a         n/a       0 orphan
      -- on the 2-D map: 20.3% ; of the neighbour lists shown: 24.6%
  Tier 0 TF-IDF, refit on 117         33.7%      51.1%       0.054     69 orphan
  Tier 1 SPECTER                      27.9%      52.5%       0.036      0 orphan
  Tier 1 PubMedBERT                   32.8%      55.7%       0.127      0 orphan
  Tier 1 all-mpnet                    32.5%      51.6%       0.137      0 orphan
  random baseline                      5.8%      20.4%          --

  "separation" = mean cosine of same-topic pairs minus mean of different-topic
  pairs. "orphan" = papers with no neighbour above min_sim 0.25.

TWO RESULTS WORTH STATING PLAINLY:

  * SPECTER LOST. It was the pre-committed strongest prior -- "purpose-built for
    scientific paper similarity from title + abstract, which is exactly this task
    ... the one to beat" -- and it came last on topic agreement, 27.9%, below
    refitted TF-IDF. Its cosines are crammed into 0.85-0.88, so almost everything
    looks similar to everything; that is what the near-zero separation says.
  * A NUMBER REPORTED EARLIER IN THIS SESSION WAS MISLEADING and is corrected
    here. Refitted Tier 0 appeared to score 65.5% on the neighbour lists the page
    shows. That was measured over only the 48 of 117 papers that had ANY neighbour
    clear the threshold -- precision at very low recall on a self-selected subset.
    On the same footing as everything else it scores 33.7%.

INPUT TEXT was the larger lever, and upstream's reasoning did not transfer.
embed.py preferred Summary alone: "the Abstract is condensed from the Summary,
there is nothing to gain from the more derived text." True of the collection it
was written for; false here, where Abstract and Summary are written separately
from reading -- what the paper did versus what it found and how.

  input (PubMedBERT)          topic     part   separation
  summary alone (upstream)    32.6%    55.9%       0.127
  + abstract                  34.0%    55.6%       0.150
  + key points                35.4%    57.6%       0.149
  + limitations  (SHIPPED)    37.1%    59.3%       0.147

  The same enrichment on all-mpnet moved topic agreement 32.5% -> 33.2%: a domain
  encoder benefits from more domain text, a general one barely does.

SHIPPED: Tier 1, NeuML/pubmedbert-base-embeddings, 768 dims, all four prose
sections. On the live page: 34.0% of each paper's five nearest DOTS share its
topic (was 20.3%), 37.3% of the neighbour lists shown (was 24.6%), and every one
of the 117 papers has a neighbour list (refitted TF-IDF left 69 with none).

A BUG WAS FOUND DOING THIS: Tier 1 had never run in this toolkit. embed.py read
model["svd"].n_components unconditionally when writing similarity.json, and Tier 1
fits no SVD. It crashed AFTER saving the model and cache but BEFORE writing
similarity.json -- so the first three runs looked successful and silently left the
previous tier's map in place, with byte-identical output. Caught only because
three different encoders produced identical scores, which is impossible.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH: that 37% is good. It is 6x better than chance and
roughly a third of each paper's nearest neighbours share its topic. Topics were
drawn to be browsable, not to be linearly separable, and several are genuinely
adjacent -- "MHC class I binding" and "Immunogenicity predictors" share most of
their vocabulary and much of their subject. The remaining levers, untested:
fine-tuning an encoder on this corpus, using citation structure rather than text,
and tuning UMAP's n_neighbors/min_dist for 117 points.
